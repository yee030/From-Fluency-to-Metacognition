2. STUDY OVERVIEW
-----------------
This study investigates how two generative AI response policies influence
first-year engineering students' early-stage design activities and
metacognitive behaviors in an introductory engineering design course.
The two experimental conditions were:

- DS (Draft-Structuring): organizes students' ideas into coherent,
  worksheet-ready drafts.
- VP (Verification-Prompting): encourages students to question premises,
  examine competing hypotheses, and identify evidence needed for
  verification before closing the problem space.

The study included 26 students randomly assigned to two conditions
(DS: n = 13; VP: n = 13). The chatbot records contain 322 student turns
(182 in DS and 140 in VP) and their corresponding AI responses.
The research also used pre/post design artifacts, behavioral coding,
survey measures, and semi-structured interviews.

3. FILES IN THIS FOLDER
-----------------------

A. Supplementary Table S1. Participant Demographics.xlsx
   Participant-level experimental metadata. The worksheet includes
   participant identifiers (P01-P26), experimental conditions, counts
   of student dialogue turns, numbers of sessions, and interview
   participation indicators. Despite the historical filename, the
   spreadsheet primarily describes experimental participation and
   interaction metadata, rather than demographic attributes such as
   age or gender. The public copy should not contain participant names
   or student identification numbers.

B. In-depth interview.xlsx
   Coded summaries of eight follow-up interview cases, including case
   identifiers, experimental conditions, interview evidence, cognitive
   transition patterns, and qualitative interpretations. Seven cases
   were included in the main qualitative interpretation; one case was
   retained as supplementary context because the recording was too
   limited for full analysis. This file contains coded summaries, not
   verbatim full-length interview transcripts.

C. student_log_chat_viewer_26_anonymized.html
   A self-contained HTML viewer for the student-AI dialogue records of
   the 26 participants. It shows the alternating student and chatbot
   messages and permits browsing and searching by pseudonymous
   participant identifier and experimental condition. Participant
   identifiers are P01-P26; DS and VP denote the two response policies.
   Direct identifiers in the original log metadata, including names
   and student numbers, have been removed from this prepared version.

4. PARTICIPANT IDENTIFIERS AND CROSS-FILE LINKAGE
--------------------------------------------------
P01-P26 are study-specific pseudonymous participant identifiers.
P01-P13 correspond to the DS condition, and P14-P26 to the VP condition.
Use the PID field to compare the participant-level metadata with the
chat records and coded interview summaries, where a matching PID is
available. No key linking PIDs to real identities is provided here.

5. HOW TO ACCESS THE MATERIALS
-----------------------------
- Open the Google Drive folder using the link in Section 1.
- Download the .xlsx files and open them in software compatible with
  Microsoft Excel spreadsheets.
- Download the .html file and open it locally in a modern web browser.
  The viewer runs locally; no special installation or server is needed.
- Select a participant to inspect student-AI conversations. Use the
  search control to locate a participant or experimental condition.

These files are research records and descriptive materials; they are
not a distribution of the original deployed chatbot application.

6. SCOPE OF REPRODUCIBILITY
---------------------------
The shared records support inspection of participant-level interaction
counts, study-condition assignments, coded interview interpretations,
and examples of students' verification and problem-definition behavior.
The manuscript and its accompanying Supplementary Information provide
additional scoring rules, statistical summaries, and analytical tables.

These three shared files alone do not necessarily provide everything
needed to rerun all reported statistical analyses or to recreate the
chatbot system. In particular, the HTML viewer is a presentation of
interaction records, not the complete chatbot source code or a set of
statistical analysis scripts. Researchers should consult the article
and Supplementary Information for methods and available analyses.

7. ETHICAL USE AND CONFIDENTIALITY
----------------------------------
This study involved human participants. Information about ethical
approval and informed consent is documented in the manuscript's
Methods section ("Ethical Approval and Informed Consent").

The public materials are intended to use pseudonymous participant IDs.
Although direct identifiers have been removed from the prepared HTML
viewer, free-text conversations may still contain contextual details
that could indirectly identify an individual. Users must not attempt
to identify participants or link these records to personal information.

Before public release, the data provider should confirm that all files,
including spreadsheet columns and free-text material, have been reviewed
for direct and indirect identifiers and that the proposed sharing is
consistent with the approved research protocol and participant consent.
